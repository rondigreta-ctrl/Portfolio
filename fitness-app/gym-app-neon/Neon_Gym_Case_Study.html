02 — READING THE MOODBOARD

What the moodboard said.

The aesthetic draws from two references:

  • —The raw, excessive energy of 80s gym culture: diamond-plate metal, neon signage, bodies pushed to the limit.
  • —Neo Acid Pop: a visual language built on fluo colour, dark surfaces, and graphic excess.

The result is an identity that feels physical, electric, and unapologetically loud.

MOODBOARD_UPDATED.PNG — Neo Acid Pop
STYLE

Neo Acid Pop

80s gym energy: excess, fluorescence, raw physicality.
The gym as a stage, not a utility space. Every visual choice screams rather than whispers.

PALETTE

Dark stage, neon performers

The dark base (#0E0E10, #17171B) is the void that makes neon possible. Lime = action. Magenta = intensity. Purple = depth.

TYPOGRAPHY

Chakra Petch — angular, technical, retrofuturist. It carries the weight of the 80s gym aesthetic without quoting it literally.

Space Mono — monospaced type reads as machine output, performance metrics, precision.

Space Grotesk — for everything in between, modern, neutral, legible.

TEXTURE & IMAGERY

Metal meets digital

Diamond-plate texture: the physicality of a real gym bleeding into the screen.
Bodies in motion, neon-lit environments, dark backgrounds. This is performance, not wellness.
The UI should feel like the gym smells. Urgent and alive.

05 — REFLECTION
  • ◇No research means every choice has to stand on its own visual merit.
  • ◇The moodboard was already set, staying faithful to it was the brief, not a starting point to question. I took it as a constraint and pushed forward.
  • ◇The unexpected challenge was accessibility. Fluo on dark looks electric, but lime and magenta don't always meet WCAG contrast requirements. Making it work within those guidelines became part of the aesthetic problem, not a separate one.
